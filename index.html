01 — Listen

Three ad slots. Three coins.

Their jar — or yours. Three times.

After each ad, you decide. Tip if the read landed — the coin goes to the podcaster with a thank-you. Jip if it didn't — the coin stays in your jar with anonymous feedback. Tap-tap-tap, three independent decisions per episode. The money flows. The data doesn't.

Empty the jar whenever.

Tips and jips both stack in your TipJip jar. Hit $20 and transfer to your bank or PayPal — no fees, no thresholds, no waiting period. Or let it ride and tip the next podcaster. Your jar, your call.

§1 · Overview

Why a protocol is needed.

By 2027, an estimated 30–40% of "viewers" on consumer platforms will be AI agents browsing on behalf of humans — researching products, watching demos, gathering information, drafting summaries. Most platforms will treat this as fraud and block it. That's the wrong move.

The right move is to formalize the distinction. Agents and humans both deliver value to advertisers — but different value. A human's emotional reaction, brand impression, and purchase intent flow from a human. An agent's information extraction, comparison, and downstream recommendation flow from a delegated process. Both are worth paying for. Neither should be priced as if it were the other.

The Agent Protocol formalizes this distinction so platforms, agents, and advertisers can interoperate without ambiguity. Honesty becomes the default state.

§2 · Identification headers

§6 · Compliance notes

What this protocol is, and isn't.

The Agent Protocol is a commercial honesty standard, not a security mechanism. It assumes vendors who attest in good faith. Vendors who falsely attest (claiming human traffic for an agent, or vice versa) face platform-level deplatforming, vendor-slug revocation, and contractual liability.

The protocol does NOT:

— attempt to detect agents that don't self-attest (that's a hopeless arms race)
— prevent agents from operating (we welcome them)
— degrade the human experience in any way
— share agent attestations with advertisers without aggregation

The protocol DOES:

— give vendors who play honestly a clear commercial path
— give advertisers a clear pricing signal
— give humans full economic priority over their own attention
— give the entire industry a shared standard to converge on
